# NULL POINTER: ANILAR SİLİNMEDEN ÖNCE — Story

> **Internal spoiler document:** This file contains the central identity reveals and ending structure. Player-facing copy must not expose this information early.

## Narrative Premise

Eren Vardar investigates the apparent suicide of Mnemosyne Systems engineer Mert Ersoy. Evidence shows that Mert's memory was manually altered shortly before his death. The case becomes personal when Eren finds a physical photograph of himself with Mert despite having no memory of knowing him.

Written on the back:

> “Dördümüzden biri hatırlamalı.”

The identifier **NLP-0417** recurs across the investigation and points toward **Project Lazarus**, a program capable of altering, reconstructing, and transferring human memory.

## Canonical Truth

These facts are fixed unless this document is deliberately revised:

1. Eren remembers himself as a police detective.
2. Eren was originally a memory researcher, not the detective he remembers being.
3. The original Eren Vardar died three years before the events of the game.
4. The biological person controlled by the player is not the original Eren.
5. A reconstruction of Eren's memory/personality was transferred into another living brain.
6. The original Eren's final memory contains evidence of large-scale illegal memory manipulation by Mnemosyne Systems.
7. That final memory is designated **NLP-0**.
8. Mert's apparent suicide and manual memory alteration are part of the route into this truth.

## Central Question

> **If your memories belong to somebody else, who are you?**

The story must not answer this with a single authorial verdict. Each ending embodies a defensible but costly answer.

## Themes

- Memory as evidence, property, injury, and identity.
- The difference between continuity and authenticity.
- Institutional control disguised as care or restoration.
- Testimony from people who remember a version of you that you cannot access.
- The ethics of exposing truth when the truth can destroy living people.
- The moral status of a reconstructed person.

## Narrative Rules

- Revelations recontextualize earlier scenes; they must not depend on unfairly withheld facts.
- The player may be misled by characters or corrupted memories, but the game must preserve discoverable contradictions.
- Eren's present consciousness is treated as a person, not merely a twist mechanism.
- Mert remains a character with motives and relationships, not just a dead exposition source.
- Technology has authored capabilities and constraints. It cannot solve arbitrary plot problems.
- `NLP-0417`, the photograph, the four-person message, Project Lazarus, and `NLP-0` are distinct clues and must not collapse into one early exposition dump.
- Optional content may deepen interpretation but cannot carry a fact required to understand the mandatory ending choice.

## Chapter Spine

### Prologue — 03:17

Purpose: establish Eren's apparent identity, the investigative tone, the interaction grammar, and a precise anomaly associated with 03:17. End with a question that makes the official account of Mert's death insufficient.

Required outcomes:

- The player can move, inspect, and record a first meaningful clue.
- Mert's death is framed as an apparent suicide, not accepted as fact.
- A subtle discontinuity establishes that perception or memory cannot be trusted completely.

### Chapter 1 — The Dead Man

Purpose: investigate Mert Ersoy as a person and establish the evidence loop. Physical evidence, testimony, and system records indicate deliberate memory alteration shortly before death.

Required outcomes:

- Mert's manual alteration is supported by more than one source.
- The player learns that a clean digital record can itself be suspicious.
- The player discovers the physical photograph of Eren with Mert and records “Dördümüzden biri hatırlamalı.”
- A short, fragmented memory glitch creates a personal breach without explaining Project Lazarus.
- `NLP-0417` enters the case without a complete explanation.

### Chapter 2 — People Who Remember You

Purpose: turn the case toward Eren. People react to him with familiarity or fear that his remembered career cannot explain. The physical photograph resists easy digital dismissal.

Required outcomes:

- The earlier photograph and message are corroborated by people who remember Eren differently.
- Eren's personal history becomes an active case thread.
- Multiple sources suggest he knew Mert.

## Implemented Opening Sequence

The current authored opening content fixes these surface-level beats without changing the later identity reveals:

1. At 03:17, Sector 7 dispatch reports Mert Ersoy's suspicious death and a memory-integrity anomaly.
2. Eren may inspect his neurological medication and a cut photograph before leaving.
3. Mert's biometric record places death at 02:36.
4. Mert's terminal records manual access at 02:51.
5. Combining those records yields the conclusion that the terminal was accessed after Mert died.
6. A physical photograph places Eren beside Mert and carries the inscription “Dördümüzden biri hatırlamalı.”
7. Collecting the photograph triggers a fragmentary laboratory/alarm memory containing Mert, an indistinct figure, and a warning that there is no return once the process begins.

The memory does not name Lazarus, identify the other figure, explain Eren's body, or disclose `NLP-0`. The damaged optional recording advises trusting the timeline rather than memory; it is foreshadowing, not proof of the central twist.

### Implemented Chapter 1 Resolution

The playable Chapter 1 investigation now extends the opening through a restrained chapter-end hook:

1. Physical implant damage and a 02:29 manual memory-deletion record support deliberate alteration from independent sources.
2. The 02:36 death time and 02:51 manual terminal access establish the first contradiction.
3. The recent mechanical door-latch use prevents the locked-room appearance from being treated as conclusive.
4. Mert's last contact attempt targeted Eren Vardar shortly before death.
5. Eren's own device has no corresponding call record.
6. `NLP-0417` links the damaged terminal file, Mert's notes, and the next investigation lead.

The implemented ending does not identify who used the terminal, prove murder, reveal Project Lazarus, disclose `NLP-0`, or resolve why Eren lacks the call. Those remain later-chapter questions.

### Chapter 3 — Lazarus

Purpose: expose the operational shape and ethical scale of Project Lazarus. Terminal investigation and memory reconstruction connect Mert, Eren, and the identifier system.

Required outcomes:

- The player understands the difference between altering, reconstructing, and transferring memory.
- Mnemosyne Systems' illegal activity is evidenced rather than merely alleged.
- The police-detective identity becomes untenable.
- `NLP-0` is established as a critical but inaccessible target.

### Chapter 4 — Who Are You?

Purpose: reveal that Eren was a memory researcher and that the original Eren died three years ago. Confirm that the player's living body is another person carrying a reconstruction.

Required outcomes:

- The reveal is cross-confirmed by independent evidence types.
- The host body's personhood and cost are acknowledged.
- The player retains agency after the reveal; investigation continues by choice, not inertia.
- The path to the original Eren's final memory becomes actionable.

### Chapter 5 — Null Pointer

Purpose: recover and interpret `NLP-0`, synthesize the case, confront the infrastructure and responsible interests, and make the final decision.

Required outcomes:

- `NLP-0` proves large-scale illegal memory manipulation.
- The risks and beneficiaries of each decision are legible.
- RELEASE, PURGE, and REMAIN are all available as intentional conclusions.
- Epilogue variations acknowledge material optional discoveries without obscuring the chosen ending.

## Ending Intent

### RELEASE

Publish the evidence. This privileges public truth and accountability while accepting the uncontrolled consequences for subjects, institutions, and Eren's own status.

### PURGE

Destroy the memory infrastructure. This prevents continued use while erasing access to memories, proof, or reconstructed selves that may depend on it.

### REMAIN

Keep the system intact and remain inside it. This preserves continuity and capability while accepting complicity, containment, or ambiguity about freedom.

No ending is labeled as the objectively good ending. Consequences must follow established capabilities and player knowledge.
